## 5. Synthesis

What the closest SUSCOM papers do that we should match
1. Descriptive titles. Titles of 101498 (15 words), 101106 (14), 101398 (colon subtitle), 101216 and 101470 (Name-colon) all name the object or method; none is finding-led. Match: a title that names the object (carbon-aware delay scheduling, HPC batch jobs, US grids). A finding-led clause would stand out; keep it under 18 words (101498, 101106, 101398; thin).
2. One-paragraph abstracts that name the data and give headline numbers: 135 and 153 words in the two full-text papers; 190 to 250 in the abstract-only set (101216, 101470, 101398, 101429). Our 220 to 250 words sits inside the range of the topic-close set, above the two fully read papers.
3. A numbered or bulleted contribution list of 3 to 4 items that ends with a code and data release (101498 releases code and data; 101398 maps contributions to gaps and sections; 101470 four bullets). Match: 3 to 4 bullets, active "we", the last one the release.
4. A roadmap sentence listing the sections by number at the end of the Introduction (101498; thin).
5. A named reproducibility element: either a Zenodo data DOI (101106) or a release statement (101498). Match: Zenodo DOI plus a Reproducibility subsection.
6. A highlights block of 3 to 5 bullets (101470 and 101398 show five and four; SUSCOM is on Elsevier, which asks for highlights; check JOURNAL_BASICS.md).
7. Section order: Introduction, Background or State of the art, Model or Methodology, Results, Discussion, Related work or Conclusion. Related work is early in 101106 and late in 101498, so either placement is accepted.

What we can do better (evidence level in brackets)
1. Uncertainty. 101498 reports mean and std from repeated noise runs; 101398 reports p values; 101106 reports std of measurement; none reports bootstrap intervals or a preregistered held-out period (full texts of 2; abstracts of 2). Our block-bootstrap intervals, preregistration and held-out year are above this bar [moderate: two full texts].
2. Forecast and runtime realism. 101498 states that its forecasts are history plus noise and that runtimes are assumed known; our real LightGBM forecast and runtime estimates remove both admitted threats [strong for 101498, weak for the others].
3. Oracle comparison. Only 101470 has an oracle (abstract). A 2x2 with an oracle on both axes goes further [moderate].
4. Multiple baselines. 101498 uses one baseline (Local). Our simple-policy ladder and best-simple-policy test is stronger [thin: one full text].
5. Real HPC traces. None of the four reads PM100, F-DATA or KIT FH2 style traces [from abstracts and one full text].

Concrete recommendations
- Title: descriptive object plus finding clause under 18 words; no Name-colon (supports: 101498, 101106, 101398, 101216, 101470).
- Abstract: single paragraph, six to eight sentences, data named, three or four numbers with intervals, one sentence on the implication that the two other topic-close abstracts lack (supports: 101498 lacks implication; 101429 ends on implication).
- Sections: Introduction (with roadmap sentence), Related work (with the comparison table), Data, Method, Results, Discussion (with trade-offs and a Threats-to-validity subsection), Reproducibility, Conclusion (supports: 101498, 101106).
- Figures: 7 to 13 appear in the two read papers; the owner's 13 to 14 is above both [thin]; captions in 101106 are descriptive; ours should state the finding, because no topic-close paper does and the owner wants it.
- Results tables: improvement versus the run-now baseline, relative and absolute, with interval; one row per policy; named baselines (101106 names its naive models; 101498 uses one baseline) [thin].
- Contribution list: three or four bullets, "We ...", last bullet the release (supports: 101498, 101398, 101470).
- Limitations: a lettered Threats-to-validity list of four to five items inside Discussion, plain admissions, about 300 to 400 words (supports: 101498; 101106 puts limits in the Conclusion). State our own assumptions (non-interruptible, no capacity limit, no migration) in the same plain way.
- Related work: cite 101498 as the paper that itself names forecast noise and known runtimes as threats, which motivates our 2x2; cite 101470 for the oracle idea (abstract-level claim only).
