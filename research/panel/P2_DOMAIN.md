## 1. Novelty
Positive: the combination in A13 s0 is unmatched in the SUSCOM corpus: real requested-versus-actual walltime (A07 median actual/requested 0.028, 0.232, 0.148), a real past-only forecaster on 42 BAs, a held-out year, a 2x2 with a Shapley split. 101470 (ReBase) has one information factor (wind forecast), unit tasks, no runtime term; 101498 has synthetic noise and known runtimes (A13 A1, A3).
Weaker than the plan suggests: NOVELTY_SCAN records that LACS models job-length error (synthetic, within 1.2 % of perfect-length), COAST uses HPC traces with known runtimes, LiteCast and EuroSys 2024 report that forecast error costs little. A hostile referee reads: "the forecast term is known; the runtime term is the only new part, and it is measured against the weakest possible runtime input."
Attack first: claim C3 and title option 1 ("jointly determine"). A Shapley split of a gap in a 2x2 is an accounting identity; the planner is one heuristic (SPEC s4.1); the runtime term is the cost of planning with R_req, a loose bound by construction (PM100 median ratio 0.028, about 36x). It measures how bad the worst credible runtime input is, not what operators can expect.
Wording that survives: "On three real HPC traces replayed on 42 US grids, a delay-to-lowest-integral planner using a past-only forecast and the requested walltime saves X to Y % of run-now emissions at 12 h slack, against Z to W % with perfect information; on PM100 and KIT FH2 the point estimate of the loss from requested walltime exceeds the loss from the forecast, on F-DATA the two are close." Avoid "jointly determine", "first", "matters more", and "retained share" in the abstract.

## 3. Baselines and arms
Present: run-now, night rule (PM100 0.29 %), seven signals including profile7, seasonal naive day and week, history profile, pred and pred2; D = lgbm|req chosen on DEV. Ledger (PM100, S12): profile7|req 1.11 %, snaive_day|req 0.96 %, histprofile|req 0.73 %, lgbm|req 1.90 %. So a simple-policy ladder exists (PREREG s7.5).
Missing, ranked by what a referee would insist on:
1. A forecast-free threshold or percentile policy (start when current intensity is below a percentile of the recent past, else wait until the slack limit). Describe as a policy "of earlier work in this family" without quoting other papers' numbers (A13/EXEMPLARS do not record such figures). Needs a PREREG_DEVIATIONS row because the scope note says no other published scheduler is reimplemented; threshold chosen on DEV. If it matches D, the paper must say so.
2. A padded or shrunk walltime rule: R_hat = c x R_req with c in {0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5} chosen on DEV. With median actual/requested 0.028 (PM100) an operator can do this without any history. X1 as written uses R_act x m (sensitivity to error); it does not test an operator-realisable rule on R_req. Add both.
3. A conservative history predictor (median or 75th percentile of the user's last 5 to 10 jobs). pred (mean of last two) is the weakest credible predictor, so H3 is a lower bound on recovery.
4. A marginal-signal run: X7 proxy, labelled as proxy, extended to lgbm arms.
5. Spatial shifting and interruptible jobs: out of scope, stated (D11/W26).
Items 1-4 fit the frozen design as labelled exploratory work: new files, own ledger, DEV-chosen parameters, separate "post-freeze, exploratory" block (SPEC s5.9, F14).

## 4. Results interpretation against the ledger (S = 12 h, energy-weighted, TEST)
Ledger values: PM100 oracle|act 5.75 %, lgbm|act 4.08, lgbm|req 1.90, lgbm|pred 2.93; Shapley forecast 1.56, runtime 2.30; retained share 0.33. F-DATA 7.22, 5.31, 3.81, 4.34; forecast 1.76, runtime 1.65; 0.53. KIT FH2 4.02, 2.82, 1.51; forecast 1.03, runtime 1.48; 0.38. H2 2.18, 1.50, 1.31 pp; H3 1.03, 0.53, 0.35 pp.
- C1 ("a minority to a half"): supported (0.33, 0.53, 0.38). Absolute savings of 1.9, 3.8 and 1.5 % of run-now emissions are small and must stand beside the relative figure in the abstract (W18, open item 13).
- C2: H2 and H3 positive on PM100 (confirmatory). H3 recovers 47 % of the H2 penalty on PM100 (1.03/2.18), 36 % on F-DATA, 27 % on KIT FH2. It is measured against the weakest baseline with the weakest predictor, so recovery is an underestimate of what a trivial rule could do.
- C3 over-claim risk, confirming the question raised in the task: Fig 7 shows runtime 60 versus forecast 40 % on PM100 (2.30 vs 1.56), 48 versus 52 % on F-DATA (1.65 vs 1.76), 59 versus 41 % on KIT FH2 (1.48 vs 1.03). On F-DATA the forecast term is larger, by 0.11 pp, with no interval (Shapley intervals pending, FF 2), and the interaction (0.29 pp F-DATA, 0.34 pp KIT FH2) is larger than that margin, so the order depends on the allocation convention. W25 wording ("two of three traces", point estimates) is acceptable only after Shapley intervals exist; "jointly determine" (title 1) and any "runtime matters more" sentence are not supported.
- The runtime term follows from looseness of requests (median ratio 0.028 PM100, 0.232 F-DATA, 0.148 KIT FH2), so the trace ordering of the term is mechanistic; show it with X1 on R_req rather than present it as a trace coincidence.
- "Retained share" has no slack-free meaning: PM100 oracle|act is 1.05 % at S2, 5.75 % at S12, 8.62 % at S24, and lgbm|req 0.26, 1.90, 2.68 %. Report absolute pp and the slack curve first.
- The forecaster's added value is modest: PM100 lgbm|act 4.08 against profile7|act 3.75 and histprofile|act 3.17; under req 1.90 against 1.11; under pred 2.93 against 2.59 (profile7|pred 2.59 from the ledger). D was chosen on DEV MAE counts (38 of 42), and MAE rank is not saving rank. Report the paired difference lgbm versus profile7 with an interval before saying a trained forecaster matters.
- Delay cost (Fig 13) accompanies the saving; give saving per hour of added delay or job slowdown for D.
- p at the bootstrap floor (1/2001, Holm 0.0015) is a statement about a conditional estimand; no significance language in the abstract.

## 5. Recommendation: MAJOR (as planned)
Scope fit and design hygiene (freeze, held-out year, ledger, leakage tests) are strong. Major because the headline runtime finding rests on the weakest runtime baseline, the confirmatory intervals are narrow relative to the job-sample uncertainty, a forecast-free threshold rule and an operator-realisable walltime rule are absent, absolute effects are small, and the C3 ordering is not yet supported by intervals.
Five changes to reach accept, ranked:
1. Add the padded-walltime (c x R_req) and conservative-history arms and make X1 (accuracy curve, on R_req and R_act) a main-text result; reframe C2 and C3 as how much of the penalty a trivial operator rule removes.
2. Add a forecast-free threshold or percentile policy and report "best simple policy versus D" as a paired difference with interval under req and pred in T4; report it honestly if D does not win.
3. Complete S1 (user-cluster) and Shapley intervals before submission; headline the wider interval; remove significance language from the abstract; state that PM100 is one 2020 trace.
4. Put absolute savings, delay cost and the slack curve ahead of retained share; reword C3 as point estimates with intervals and drop "jointly determine" from the title (prefer title option 2).
5. Close realism gaps as exploratory: S2 for D and the best simple policy; X7 for lgbm arms; a clock-placement sensitivity; an explicit "bound under free capacity and non-interruptible jobs" in the abstract; Kestrel absence stated up front.
