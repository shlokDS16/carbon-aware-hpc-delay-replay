## Abstract

In a cloud computing environment, job scheduling allows the service provider to schedule resources based on demand. Job scheduling must also ensure QoS, end-user satisfaction, and the efficient usage of resources. Cloud computing vendors assign virtualized computing resources to end-users based on job requirements that are dynamically scalable and pay-per-use. The assignment of jobs requires proper investigation and mapping of available resources. In this paper, we have proposed a novel job scheduling scheme based on Rock Hyrax. Our Rock Hyrax approach uses objective functions to map jobs to available resources. The objective function considers a variety of QoS parameters like makespan, response time and energy efficiency. Our method employs two key QoS parameters: makespan and energy consumption. The node behavior and characteristics, such as processing power, storage, and network connectivity to cluster similar resources, have also been considered for scheduling. An experimental setup is created for a thorough study of the proposal using CloudSim simulator. For both the jobs and virtual machines, static and dynamic scenarios for performance evaluation have been developed. To compare our work with existing scheduling algorithms like ACO, PSO, BFO, and ABC has been considered and we have found that the proposal reduces makespan by 2'9% as increased in jobs. Furthermore, the proposed method reduces total energy consumption in data centers by 7'23% as jobs request increases. The findings support the claim that the proposed method surpasses the existing methods and significantly shortens the time needed to determine the resource required for the job.

Rock Hyrax are tiny animals that are vegetarian in diet and are native to Africa and the Middle East [52]. A Rock Hyrax group has 80'100 animals and employs a divide-and-conquer strategy for foraging to defend other members while observing their surroundings [53]. The foraging process is carried out by male Rock Hyrax and it alerts the other members of the group if foraging is successful. Foraging practices are limited to keep members safe. Rock Hyrax communicates by making distinct noises, each with a different message. One sound may be for alarm, while another may be for food. The Rock Hyrax method is used in the proposal to optimize the scheduling algorithm. A process flow illustrated in Fig. 4 helps in a better understanding of the proposal.

In the initial phase of the algorithm, the resources are clustered into three clusters. As a new job is submitted to the broker, the broker passes it to the scheduler for scheduling. At scheduler, the Rock Hyrax algorithm is called. The algorithm initializes the objective function and the Rock Hyrax population in the search space. As the proposed algorithm is based on the divide and conquer strategy, the subgroups are created

from the initial population. In each subgroup, a Hyrax is chosen for foraging purposes. The fitness value of each chosen Hyrax is determined, and the Hyrax that has the maximum value is elected as the Universal Leader.

The fitness function is calculated after every iteration, and if the value is better, the Hyrax moves ahead. The iterations are continued until the stop condition is met. If the result is optimal, the algorithm returns the fitness value; otherwise, choose another Hyrax for the same process. ## Conclusions and future works

To schedule jobs, a Rock Hyrax optimization-based scheduling approach using clustering is proposed in the paper. The proposed method uses the K-means clustering algorithm to divide resources into clusters of computation, storage, and network. The proposed natureinspired algorithm schedules the job in a cloud environment. The

proposed algorithm reduces the makespan and energy efficiency by balancing resource exploitation and exploration while scheduling jobs to available resources. During scheduling, the proposed method maintains work deadlines while keeping makespan to a minimum. This is accomplished by considering the speed of VMs and the type of resource demanded by the task. The performance of the proposed algorithm was compared to existing algorithms like ACO, PSO, BFO, and ABC, and it

was clear from previous literature that the proposal reduces average makespan of 6.09% as the jobs increased from 10 to 100. Furthermore, the proposed method reduces average energy consumption in data centers by 15.8% when increased in job from 10 to 100. In this work, we have only considered QoS parameters. Whereas we did not consider the network and computing cost involve in our proposed approach. The proposed method can also be incorporated in edge computing scenario where the edge nodes are having limited computing capacity. In future, we are planning to apply our approach in small microservices applications over edge computing environments.
