## Abstract

The high energy consumption of cloud data centers (DCs) leads to a substantial carbon footprint. By reducing reliance on carbon-intensive fuels, renewable energy sources (RESs) such as wind power help mitigate greenhouse gas emissions. However, the inherent intermittency and fluctuation of RES generation, coupled with the stochastic nature of workload arrivals, complicate real-time scheduling and thereby significantly limit RES utilization efficiency in DCs. To address these issues, we propose a forecast-driven two-stage workload scheduling scheme that improves both scheduling efficiency and environmental sustainability. Specifically, we design a forecasting framework that integrates long short-term memory (LSTM) variants with a hierarchical decomposition using empirical mode decomposition (EMD) followed by variational mode decomposition (VMD). By precisely eliminating high-frequency noise and separately forecasting frequency components, the framework reduces noise interference and more accurately captures temporal patterns in workload and RES series. In the first stage, based on these forecasting results, effective global optimization is achieved in offline scheduling. In the second stage, scheduling results are dynamically adjusted based on real-time RES supply and workload demand to correct prediction errors. Experiments on real-world datasets validate the effectiveness of the proposed scheme. The forecasting models consistently outperform multiple baselines in prediction accuracy, achieving 3.41-69.46% reductions in mean absolute error compared to the state-of-the-art method. In addition, the proposed scheduling scheme increases RES utilization by 17.73'40.40% and achieves a corresponding 8.55-16.27 tons reduction in carbon emissions compared with the baselines. Furthermore, it shortens real-time scheduling latency by 81.3% relative to the real-time-only variant, underscoring its effectiveness in enabling sustainable and efficient DC operations.

## Introduction

As essential components of modern information technology infrastructure, cloud data centers (DCs) provide advanced computational, storage, and analytical capabilities. They support applications across diverse domains, such as large-scale data analysis, computational and graphics processing, and artificial intelligence. However, the widespread utilization of DCs has positioned them as energyintensive consumers, with substantial environmental impacts. For example, Google's carbon emissions reached 14.3 million tons in 2023, a nearly 50% increase over five years [1]. International Energy Agency estimates that global DC power consumption could rise from about 460 TWh in 2022 to over 1000 TWh by 2026, almost doubling and reaching a level comparable to Japan's total electricity demand [2].

In the wake of increasing concerns over carbon emissions, countries such as Sweden, Switzerland, and Canada have taken the lead in implementing carbon taxes to internalize the cost of carbon emissions into economic activities [3]. Besides, countries like the United States, Canada, and Australia have implemented voluntary industry standards and certification mechanisms, such as the Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design certification and the Energy Star label [4], to encourage DCs to adopt energy efficiency measures and reduce carbon emissions. In addition, Google, Microsoft, and other corporations actively set their own net-zero carbon emission targets and established distributed DC networks across regions abundant in renewable energy sources (RESs) to lower the carbon footprint [5]. In China, the government launched the ''East-West Computing Resource Transfer''

project, which promotes the migration of computing tasks from eastern to western DCs to harness the abundant land and RESs in the western region and to alleviate the computing demand pressure in the east [6]. RESs offer an effective means of reducing carbon emissions [7, 8]. However, their inherent variability introduces considerable uncertainty into the power supply, limiting RES utilization efficiency. Meanwhile, workload arrivals in DC is stochastic and time-varying, further complicating scheduling decisions. Therefore, accurately forecasting both RES generation and workload arrivals is critical to mitigating supply'demand mismatch. In this paper, we propose a forecastdriven two-stage workload scheduling scheme (F2S-WSS). In the first stage, high-precision forecasting of workload arrivals and wind power (WP) generation is conducted to enable global optimization in offline scheduling. In the second stage, real-time adjustments are made to correct the pre-allocated results based on actual WP supply and workload demand, thereby improving both scheduling efficiency and environmental sustainability. The major contributions of this paper are as follows:

' The proposed F2S-WSS leverages high-accuracy forecasting to guide flexible workload allocation that actively responds to WP's spatiotemporal variations, thereby improving WP utilization and reducing carbon emissions.

' The F2S-WSS transforms traditional real-time scheduling into a two-stage decision process by combining offline global optimization with real-time error correction, which reduces online complexity and enhances system-wide performance under uncertainty.

' A hierarchical data preprocessing strategy is employed to mitigate high-frequency noise interference in forecasting. Empirical mode decomposition (EMD) decomposes the input data, and variational mode decomposition (VMD) refines the high-frequency component to selectively suppress noise-dominated parts for precise denoising.

' To further improve prediction accuracy, long short-term memory (LSTM) variants are applied to individually forecast each decomposed frequency component, enabling finer modeling of temporal patterns.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: Section 2 discusses the related work in the field. Section 3 gives detailed descriptions of the prediction model used in this study and the mathematical formulation for the proposed scheduling problem; Section 4 describes the proposed algorithm; Section 5 introduces the simulation setting and analyzes the numerical results; and Section 6 concludes the paper.

## Related work

With growing concerns over the high energy consumption and carbon emissions of cloud DCs, carbon-aware cloud resource management has attracted significant attention [9]. Its core idea is to exploit the spatiotemporal variability of RESs for dynamic workload scheduling, thereby mitigating the mismatch between computational demand and power supply. Existing research can generally be categorized into three areas: spatiotemporal coordination across geo-distributed DCs, edge/fog'cloud collaboration, and green federated learning (FL). In the cross'DC context, prior studies mainly explored intelligent scheduling to dynamically match computing workloads with RES availability. For instance, [10] proposed an energy-aware task scheduling method based on reinforcement learning (RL), combining Q-learning with a deep Q-network to derive optimal policies that balance performance and energy consumption under dynamic workload arrivals. However, single-agent RL struggles to meet the distributed and adaptive requirements of multi'DC green scheduling; to overcome this, [11] introduced a multi-agent RL framework with a hierarchical structure of global and local agents. By extending the actor'critic model to incorporate other

agents' actions and observations during training, this framework enables cooperative scheduling and enhances RES utilization efficiency. In edge/fog'cloud collaboration, researchers have explored hierarchical architectures to enhance energy efficiency and support low-carbon operation. For example, [12] proposed an end'edge'cloud control method in which end devices handle data collection, edge nodes conduct selfoptimized tuning, and the cloud layer adapts models. Validated in industrial scenarios, this multi-layer collaboration effectively achieved low-carbon operation. Similarly, [13] developed a hierarchical cloud' fog architecture that combines global optimization in the cloud with near-source responsiveness in the fog. This framework reduced response time and energy consumption. At the application layer, studies have extended carbon-awareness to FL to enable green artificial intelligence (AI) training [14]. The proposed carbon-conscious federated RL framework adaptively optimizes client selection and resource usage via RL, balancing carbon efficiency with model performance.

Reliable forecasting of RES generation and workload demand is essential for optimizing scheduling strategies under uncertainty, and has become a central focus of current research [15'17]. Existing forecasting approaches typically fall into two major categories: (1) increasing model structural complexity, and (2) improving input data quality via data preprocessing techniques. For example, in [18], an LSTM network is utilized to predict WP from multiple turbines and integrate it with the attraction'repulsion optimization algorithm to optimize the LSTM training process, significantly improving the model's prediction accuracy. In [19], the performance of stacked and simplified LSTM models is investigated for solar power forecasting, showing that the stacked LSTM achieves higher accuracy by capturing complex temporal dependencies more effectively. In [20], an intelligent computing framework is proposed for predicting cloud virtual machine resources using multivariate time-series forecasting based on bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM). The study also compares the performance of various deep learning models, such as LSTM, BiLSTM, and stacked BiLSTM. In [21], a hybrid model based on LSTM and attention mechanism (ALSTM) is proposed, which enhances the LSTM neural network's ability to capture key features by adaptively focusing on the importance of input features, thereby significantly improving the forecasting accuracy of photovoltaic power.

High-frequency noise in training data often degrades forecasting accuracy by impairing temporal feature learning. Signal processing techniques, such as signal decomposition and reconstruction, are therefore widely employed to suppress noise and extract salient features from raw sequences [22]. WP generation and workload arrivals can also be treated as nonstationary signals, and decomposing them into sub-sequences of different frequency characteristics has been shown to improve prediction performance. For instance, [23] applies empirical wavelet transform (EWT) to decompose wind speed data into multiple intrinsic mode components, which are then predicted individually using an LSTM-based model, and the reconstructed sum of these subsequences is used as the final forecast. In [24], EMD is employed to separate the input signal into several intrinsic mode functions (IMFs) and a residual component, thereby facilitating more effective feature extraction and enhancing forecasting accuracy. Building on this, [25] adopts VMD to extract frequency-based modal components, which are modeled separately and subsequently combined. These decomposition techniques have shown effectiveness in improving forecasting accuracy. Nevertheless, residual high-frequency noise and mode mixing often persist, obscuring temporal patterns and risking the loss of useful features during denoising. Enhanced variants such as complete ensemble EMD with adaptive noise (CEEMDAN) [26] alleviate mode mixing by adding adaptive noise into the residual signal, yet their performance remains limited by the single-layer design. In particular, high-frequency noise often concentrates in a few IMFs, so excessive denoising risks losing key features, while preserving features leaves the noise insufficiently suppressed. Lacking a targeted refinement mechanism, single-layer decomposition fails to balance global

trend preservation and local noise reduction. To address these challenges, this paper introduces a dual-layer decomposition strategy that first applies global signal decomposition and then performs targeted refinement on the most noise-contaminated components. This stepwise process preserves dominant features while adaptively suppressing noise, thereby improving feature extraction and forecasting accuracy. Specifically, we adopt a hierarchical EMD-VMD framework, where EMD provides adaptive global decomposition and VMD performs refined analysis on noise-prone components, thus combining their complementary strengths.

Although forecasting of RES generation and workload arrivals has advanced substantially, their systematic integration into scheduling remains limited in much of the prior literature. To better exploit forecasts, research attention has been gradually shifting toward a two-stage framework: Stage I (day-ahead) generates RES and load forecasts and computes an offline pre-allocation; Stage II (intraday/real-time) performs re-dispatch based on realized RES and demand to correct forecast deviations. For example, [27] applies solar and load forecasts to microgrid scheduling, achieving cost reduction and improved reliability, but treats forecasted values as actual ones, overlooking prediction errors. Similarly, [28] employs workload prediction to guide scheduling in fog/cloud environments, yet its fault-tolerance mechanism focuses on task replication to improve completion rate, while neglecting prediction errors and RES utilization. [29] proposed a two-stage optimization control method for microgrid energy management, where the first stage performs economic dispatch based on forecast data and the second stage adjusts the schedule with real-time information to smooth prediction errors. While this approach highlights the importance of correcting forecast deviations, it does not investigate forecasting methods and instead assumes fixed error ranges (e.g., '5% for load and '20% for wind/PV), leaving the integration between forecasting and scheduling insufficiently addressed. Compared with these studies, our work introduces two main improvements. First, we enhance forecasting accuracy by incorporating a hierarchical EMD'VMD preprocessing step prior to LSTM prediction. This improvement directly addresses the insufficient forecasting techniques in [27,28], and also complements [29], which overlooks prediction modeling. Second, in the real-time scheduling stage, we correct forecast errors to improve scheduling robustness and overall system reliability. This not only remedies the lack of error handling in [27,28], but also extends [29] by providing a systematic integration of forecasting and error correction.

## Algorithm design

A forecast-driven two-stage workload scheduling scheme is proposed to enhance WP utilization under uncertainty through flexible workload allocation. The F2S-WSS divides the scheduling process into two phases: an offline phase that performs global optimization based on forecasted workload and WP generation, and a real-time phase that adjusts pre-allocated resources to correct forecast errors and adapt to real-time supply'demand dynamics.

As shown in Fig. 3, the scheduling process is supported by a data preprocessing and forecasting pipeline. Specifically, to improve data quality, the original WP generation and workload arrival data are first processed using an EMD-VMD-based hierarchical decomposition to suppress high-frequency noise. Each decomposed component is then predicted independently using LSTM variants, enabling finer temporal modeling. The aggregated forecasts of all components are combined to form the inputs for the subsequent two-stage scheduling process. The following subsections describe each phase in further detail.

The prediction module generates forecasts of workload and WP, which serve as inputs to the F2S-WSS. As illustrated in the forecasting block of Fig. 3, the process consists of seven main steps: data partition, time series windowing, standard scaler normalization, LSTM-based model training, point-by-point prediction, inverse normalization, and final aggregation of the predicted IMFs to produce the complete forecast. This procedure is applied independently to each IMF component obtained from the preceding data preprocessing phase.

Each IMF component is treated as a one-dimensional time-series sequence (e.g., historical workload arrivals or WP generation). A fixedlength sliding window is first applied to segment the sequence into input'output pairs for point-by-point prediction, ensuring the preservation of temporal dependencies. Subsequently, both training and testing samples are normalized using standard z-score normalization to enhance numerical stability and accelerate convergence during model training.

Four types of LSTM-based forecasting models ' LSTM, Stacked LSTM, BiLSTM, and ALSTM ' are comparatively evaluated, as shown in Fig. 2. The LSTM architecture processes the sequential input through a single layer of memory cells and outputs the predicted value via a fully connected (dense) layer. The Stacked LSTM architecture deepens the model by stacking two LSTM layers. The output of the first layer serves as the input to the second, allowing for hierarchical temporal feature extraction. The final hidden state from the second layer is passed through a linear dense layer to produce the forecast. The BiLSTM model captures both forward and backward temporal dependencies. At each time step, hidden states from the forward and backward LSTM passes are concatenated, and the combined representation is used to generate

the output. The ALSTM architecture augments an LSTM with a featurewise attention gate; for a fair comparison with the other three models, the attention is applied only to the last hidden state, and the gated representation is then fed to a fully connected (dense) layer to produce the forecast.

All models are trained using mean squared error (MSE) as the loss function. Once trained, the models predict the test set sequences for each IMF. The results are then inverse-normalized and aggregated to reconstruct the final forecasts for workload and WP. In practical deployment, forecasting models adopt a hybrid periodic and eventtriggered retraining policy to ensure accuracy without unnecessary overhead. Periodic updates capture regular patterns, with workload

models retrained daily or weekly and WP models aligned with external forecast cycles, while event triggers initiate fine-tuning or full retraining when significant drift is detected.

## Conclusion

This paper presents a forecast-driven workload scheduling scheme named F2S-WSS to enhance the sustainability and operational efficiency of wind-powered cloud DCs. To address the challenges of WP variability and workload uncertainty, the F2S-WSS transforms traditional real-time scheduling into a two-stage decision process. In the first stage, a forecasting framework integrates hierarchical decomposition EMD-VMD and LSTM variants to suppress high-frequency noise and model temporal patterns across frequency bands, enabling accurate prediction of WP generation and workload arrivals for global offline scheduling. In the second stage, real-time adjustments are performed to correct pre-allocated results based on actual WP availability and workload demand, thereby improving adaptability. Extensive simulations on real-world datasets validate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed scheme. Ablation study. The ablation results further validate the benefits of offline prediction-based scheduling. Compared with the real-time only variant, WP utilization and penetration increase by 19.28% and 18.89%, accompanied by a reduction of 9.17 tons in carbon emissions. In addition, runtime decreases by 81.3%, since a significant portion of workload allocation is pre-processed offline, thereby reducing the computational burden of online scheduling.

Scalability. When scaling to 10 DCs (with workload and resource increased), F2S-WSS demonstrates availability proportionally controlled runtime growth and even higher WP penetration.
