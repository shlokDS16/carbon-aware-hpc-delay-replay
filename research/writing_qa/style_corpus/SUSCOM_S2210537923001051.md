## Abstract

Under the background of "carbon neutrality ", data center enterprises are confronted with the challenges of high energy costs and the need to manage carbon emissions. Compared with traditional energy sources, renewable energy possesses the advantages of being low-carbon and cost-effective, making it an essential avenue for data centers to enhance their utilization of renewable energy. By employing a spatio-temporal scheduling method for computing power load, data center enterprises can maximize the benefits of renewable energy, achieve lowcarbon and cost-effective operation, and enhance the consumption of renewable energy. This study developed a spatio-temporal scheduling model for computing load in data centers, with a specific focus on optimizing the utilization of renewable energy while considering the goals of low-carbon emissions and cost-effectiveness. A two-stage spatio-temporal scheduling algorithm (ESTS) was designed and implemented, and three sets of experiments were conducted to assess the effectiveness and applicability of offline load scheduling using offline load data from Alibaba's cluster-trace-v2018. The results demonstrate that the proposed scheduling method can achieve a significant reduction of carbon emissions by 70% and operating costs by 40% across various scenarios. Moreover, during the summer season when renewable energy is abundant, the application of this scheduling method in a single data center can effectively achieve the objectives of managing low-carbon emissions and minimizing costs.

## Introduction

Over the past years, the size of data centers has been increasing significantly. Since the essence of the data center is to convert electricity into computing power, the increase in computing power inevitably leads to increased energy consumption. In 2020, the power consumption of Chinese data centers will account for 2% of the whole society. The average energy consumed by each data center is almost equal to the energy consumed by 25,000 homes in the U.S. [1]. For large companies like Google, a mere 3% reduction in energy costs can result in savings of over a million dollars [2]. With the global push towards "carbon neutrality", the management of carbon emissions in data centers has become a pressing issue.[3]. To address the challenge of high carbon emissions, the use of carbon-free renewable energy has emerged as a viable solution [3], with the renewable energy industry experiencing rapid development. From 2010 to 2020, the cost of photovoltaic power generation dropped by 85%, making it cheaper to operate than that of

coal-fired power generation [4]. Similarly, wind power generation also boasts an extremely low cost [5]. The cost-effectiveness of renewable energy has become a crucial factor driving data centers to explore its utilization. Consequently, the strategic consumption of renewable energy can simultaneously achieve the goals of low-carbon and cost-effective data center operations, presenting new opportunities for optimizing data center operations.

However, as intermittent and volatile energy sources, wind power generation and solar power generation pose significant challenges when directly integrated into the grid utility. The integration of these renewable energy sources, once their proportion exceeds 3%, can significantly impact dispatching control, safe operation, and maintenance of the existing grid utility [6]. This limitation greatly hampers the development and cross-regional consumption of renewable energy in the western regions [7]. Effective utilization of renewable energy by data centers can not only benefit power companies and data centers economically [8], but also reduce carbon dioxide emissions [9].

Nevertheless, there are still numerous obstacles to increasing the consumption of renewable energy in data centers. On the power supply side, the volatility of renewable energy leads to unstable supply quantities. While grid utility power is stable, the carbon intensity of power varies across regions due to different energy generation structures. Consequently, there are differences in electricity sourced from different regions, as well as discrepancies between the concepts of "carbon emissions" and "renewable energy consumption". In terms of computing power requirements, load scheduling must consider multiple constraints, such as service quality requirements and server capacity limitations. Moreover, computing power loads exhibit great diversity with online loads (e.g., web games) requiring millisecond-level latency to provide an optimal interactive experience, while offline loads (e.g., data analysis) can tolerate several hours of delay without negative consequences based on business needs. Usually, offline loads have a long execution window that the user sets and can be completed at any point before the deadline. As a result, offline loads can be planned for execution during times when there is a high concentration of renewable energy (temporal flexibility), and these workloads can be moved to other data centers with high renewable energy availability (spatial flexibility), optimizing the effectiveness of the data center's use of renewable energy. Therefore, the challenge lies in how to effectively schedule resources to meet diverse quality of service (QoS) requirements while maximizing the benefits of renewable energy consumption, given the time and spatial flexibility of data center computing power loads[10, 11].

In this comprehensive context, it becomes crucial to design a scheduling method that leverages the time and spatial flexibility of data center computing loads [12] to achieve economic and low-carbon benefits while promoting the consumption of renewable energy. Specifically, the goal is to incorporate renewable energy consumption into the operational and management objectives of data centers' low-carbon transformation.

Existing research primarily focuses on how data centers can enhance low-carbon or economic benefits through renewable energy consumption. However, solely tracking renewable energy from a spatial dimension tends to yield suboptimal scheduling results. Furthermore, most studies overlook the significant impact of renewable energy consumption on the goal of "carbon neutrality" and fail to consider it in data center load scheduling objectives. This paper aims to address these gaps and make the following contributions: (i) in terms of scheduling optimization objectives, the paper focuses on renewable energy consumption and proposes a multi-objective load scheduling optimization model that minimizes carbon emissions, operating costs, and maximizes renewable energy consumption; (ii) in terms of problem-solving, the paper considers the tracking of renewable energy from both temporal and spatial dimensions, and designs a two-stage spatio-temporal algorithm for computing power load scheduling (ESTS); (iii) the validity and applicability of the algorithm are tested and analyzed using real computing power demand data (offline load data from Alibaba's clustertrace-v2018).

The remaining sections of this paper are organized as follows: Section 2 discusses the related work in the field. Section 3 presents the problem of Spatio-temporal load scheduling with tracking renewable energy, including the evaluation of computing power load scheduling benefits and the Spatio-temporal scheduling model of computing load. Section 4 introduces the Spatio-temporal scheduling algorithm composed of two-stage algorithms for tracking renewable energy. Section 5 analyzes the validity and applicability of the Spatio-temporal scheduling method through experiments. Finally, Section 6 concludes with a summary.

The idea behind load space scheduling in data centers is to distribute the load among different regions based on renewable energy generation and carbon emissions, aiming to achieve optimization goals. As research progresses, scholars have discovered that by scheduling computing power loads in time and space and tracking renewable energy, the potential of renewable energy in reducing data center operating costs and carbon emissions can be maximized. The Spatiotemporal scheduling strategy of computing power load adds timebased scheduling to the existing space-based scheduling strategy. In the same time period, the goal is to distribute as much load as possible to areas with high comprehensive benefits and allocate as much load as possible in the same area during periods of high comprehensive benefits. Yang Ting [18] designed a Spatio-temporal scheduling model that matches computing power load with renewable energy on a large time scale. This model allocates the computing power load to different data centers and dynamically updates the processing queue of offline loads within the data center to maximize the use of renewable energy and reduce carbon emissions. This study highlights the advantages of computing power load space-conditioning in promoting the consumption of renewable energy and reducing carbon emissions in data centers. However, it does not consider how data centers can convert the low-cost attributes of renewable energy into economic benefits through computing load scheduling. Yuan et al.[19,20] also studied the Spatio-temporal scheduling problem of distributed data centers. They addressed the energy cost minimization problem in each time slot through a nonlinear constrained optimization approach. Specifically, a genetic simulated-annealing-based particle swarm optimization method was proposed to tackle this problem. However, a notable drawback of this study is its exclusive focus on price optimization, neglecting the crucial aspects of carbon emissions and renewable energy sources. By not considering these factors, the proposed approach may not provide a comprehensive solution for achieving sustainable and environmentally friendly energy management. Additionally, it is worth noting that this algorithm may not be suitable for current large-scale distributed data centers that operate continuously, as it may raise concerns regarding efficiency.

The two-stage algorithm for computing power load space-time scheduling in data centers (ESTS) proposed in this paper consists of the computing load space scheduling algorithm (ESS) and the computing power load space-time migration algorithm (ESTS') for offline loads. The algorithm principle is illustrated in Fig. 2.

The overall idea of the algorithm is to first use the ESS algorithm to perform preliminary scheduling of online and offline requests, and then use the ESTS' algorithm for secondary scheduling of offline requests. The difference between online and offline requests lies in the deadline, where requests exceeding a single scheduling time are considered offline requests. The complexity of the ESS algorithm in each iteration is O(n), while the complexity of the ESTS' algorithm is also O(n).

The principle of the ESTS algorithm is to schedule non-delayable online loads in space for maximum efficiency, and to schedule delayable offline loads in both time and space for maximum efficiency. Specifically, the algorithm searches for the data center with the highest

comprehensive benefits (carbon emissions and operating costs) and performs spatial scheduling on the online load. It also performs initial spatial scheduling on the offline load, where the comprehensive benefit is measured in a weighted manner. The weighting coefficient is determined based on experimental results from literature [27]. Next, the algorithm searches for data centers with high renewable energy consumption efficiency within the delay constraints of offline loads and server capacity constraints, and performs a second scheduling of offline loads. The calculation of the consumption benefit of renewable energy is divided into formulas (17) and (18), which determine the priority of energy in different regions: "Renewable Energy with High Comprehensive Benefits" >"Renewable Energy with Low Comprehensive Benefits" >"Grid utility electricity with High Comprehensive Benefits" >"Grid utility electricity with Low Comprehensive Benefits". The algorithm allows for multiple combinations of computing power load optimization objectives and exhibits strong scalability.

The order of ESS and ESTS' in the ESTS algorithm is determined by two factors. First, the computing power demand side of the data center has diverse load requirements, and the execution time of online loads is less flexible and tightly constrained. Therefore, prioritizing the allocation of scarce renewable energy, which is volatile, to online loads is the most efficient scheduling method. Second, renewable energy at the power supply end is volatile. Although there are small time zone differences in different regions of the country, there is no significant difference in the temporal fluctuation of energy in different regions. For example, photovoltaic resources are available between 11 am and 5 pm. However, there are significant differences in the spatial fluctuations of energy resources at different times. For instance, the wind resources in Ningxia are significantly greater than those in other regions.

The second stage of the ESTS algorithm is the ESTS' algorithm, which aims to reschedule the delayable load in time and space within the delay constraints and server scale constraints of the data center. This stage is based on the spatial scheduling obtained from the ESS stage. The pseudocode for this stage is as follows: 1. ## Result

In the result section, this paper addresses the demand for highenergy-consuming data centers to reduce operating costs and carbon emissions by consuming renewable energy under the "double carbon" background. It proposes a data center computing power load scheduling management strategy considering the consumption of renewable energy, which explores the potential of renewable energy consumption in improving the economic and low-carbon efficiency of data centers. The experimental verification confirms the effectiveness and applicability of the two-stage computing load scheduling algorithm proposed in this paper in reducing carbon emissions, lowering operating costs, and increasing renewable energy consumption targets.

Based on this research, future exploration can be conducted in two aspects. Firstly, it can investigate how to increase the consumption of renewable energy through load scheduling after incorporating energy storage devices, further realizing the low-carbon and low-cost management of data centers. Secondly, it can explore the coordination between the user side and the energy supply side through the computing power load scheduling method from the perspective of the data center after adding the demand response mechanism of the data center computing power service.
