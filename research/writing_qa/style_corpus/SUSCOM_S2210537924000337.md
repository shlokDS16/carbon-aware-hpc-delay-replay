## Abstract

The Internet of Things (IoT) is constantly evolving and expanding. However, due to the limited IoT resources, it is intertwined with fog computing to use their resources to compensate for the limitations of IoT resources. On the other hand, fog devices face challenges, such as resource heterogeneity, high distribution, dynamism, and limitations, so an efficient task scheduling approach is needed to deploy fog computing resources effectively and improve the quality of service (QoS). This work mathematically formulates the task scheduling problem to minimize energy consumption and cost and improve QoS by reducing response time and deadline violation times of IoT tasks. Then, it proposes an Energy-efficient and deadline-Aware Task scheduling in Fog Computing (ETFC) method that predicts the traffic of fog nodes by a Support Vector Machine (SVM) and divides them into lowtraffic and high-traffic groups. Next, the ETFC method schedules the low-traffic part with an algorithm based on reinforcement learning using the proposed ICLA-SOA, which is an algorithm based on irregular cellular learning automata and schedules the tasks of the high-traffic part with a metaheuristic algorithm using the proposed Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm (NSGA-III). The simulation results demonstrate that the ETFC method exhibits up to an 84 % enhancement in response time, up to a 33 % reduction in energy consumption, up to a 30 % decrease in costs, and up to a 28 % advancement in meeting task deadlines compared to other methods.

## Introduction

The Internet of Things (IoT) is a technology in the field of the Internet and networks [1,2] that was proposed in recent decades. The IoT is a system of interconnected physical devices, such as vehicles and appliances, that are equipped with sensors, software, and internet connectivity [3]. These smart devices can collect and share data autonomously. It encompasses a wide range of interconnected devices, from simple smart home gadgets to complex industrial machinery, and transportation systems [4'6].

The IoT has been welcomed and is growingly expanding due to its high-quality services. At present, approximately 62 billion objects are linked together, with projections suggesting that this figure will increase to 75 billion by 2025 [7]. The International Data Company (IDC) predicts that by 2025, there will be 55 trillion connected devices globally, which includes 550 million current sensors and 76 billion sensor-enabled items connected to networks [8].

IoT applications are typically sensitive to delays and have necessarily strict requirements for quality of service (QoS). Cloud computing and fog computing have been proposed to solve this problem [11]. However, running various IoT applications on cloud servers comes with its own set of obstacles, including issues like traffic congestion, significant round-trip delays, security issues [12,13], and higher energy consumption during data transmission and processing [14]. Additionally, transmitting the vast volume of data generated by IoT devices to the cloud demands substantial bandwidth and can be a time-intensive process [15].

To tackle these challenges and prioritize latency in IoT, Cisco introduced the idea of Fog Computing [16], where the fog was implemented by executing applications on their devices. Fog computing offers computational and storage capabilities at the edge of the IoT network [17]. Delay is much lower in fog computing than in cloud computing because computational resources are near IoT devices in the former, so it is more economical than the latter in terms of delay, energy

consumption, and bandwidth. Although fog computing is a good solution for processing IoT programs, the use of IoT in fog still faces some challenges. Fog computing resources are heterogeneous, have limited capacity, are highly dynamic, and are distributed across the network [18]. Thus, the supply and management of fog resources are vital for fully grasping its capabilities.

However, fog computing faces various limitations in terms of resource scheduling and allocation [15], including the restricted resources of nodes, varying specifications from different manufacturers, and interference among distributed loads. Fog nodes, which are typically mobile terminal devices or small-scale network equipment, have limited capacities for data calculation, storage, and communication compared to the efficient and powerful processing capacity of cloud center devices [19,20], which results in limited resources for user devices. On the other hand, as the application expands and more components are incorporated, the number of fog nodes may also increase. However, it is essential to schedule the existing fog nodes efficiently, considering their diversity [21]. Therefore, developing an efficient strategy for allocating the limited resources of fog nodes is crucial in achieving utility optimization for the entire fog computing system to meet the needs of user devices, and deadlines, reduce costs [22], and improve QoS [23].

A deadline in IoT refers to the specific time by which a task or operation needs to be completed. Meeting task deadlines is essential for real-time applications, where timely responses are critical [18]. Examples include healthcare monitoring [24], industrial automation [25], intelligent agricultural systems [26], and autonomous vehicles [27], Intelligent transportation systems [28]. Similarly, meeting deadlines is crucial in IoT applications to ensure that real-time data processing and communication are carried out effectively and without delays [18]. Meeting task deadlines directly impacts QoS. If tasks are completed within their specified time frames, the system will maintain responsiveness and reliability. Failure to meet deadlines can lead to inefficiencies, errors, and disruptions in the functioning of IoT systems.

In addition to QoS, energy consumption is a critical issue that must be optimized when using cloud computing [29]. In fog networks like other networks, such as software-defined networks [30], wireless sensor networks [31], mobile ad hoc networks [32], and cloud workflow scheduling [33], the objective is to minimize energy consumption for real-time applications by efficiently processing and transmitting information, which involves reducing energy usage for processing data within local computers and transmitting data over fog networks. Additionally, offloading applications on centralized cloud servers consumes significant energy. Distributed fog computing aims to optimize application transmission energy for small-sized sensing and actuating devices. One method to achieve this is through intelligent workload scheduling, which involves scheduling, allocating, or migrating tasks to servers in a way that minimizes energy consumption [34].

Accordingly, we need a solution for effective resource allocation in IoT to reduce energy consumption without violating task deadlines. In this respect, many researchers have proposed various resource scheduling strategies [18,35]. Resource scheduling in the fog is an NP-hard problem, and finding a precise algorithm and an ideal solution for it will not be easy [36,37], especially when resources and tasks are numerous [38]. This reflects the significance of optimal and efficient techniques for solving scheduling problems in fog computing. Metaheuristic solutions have often been suggested to solve the NP-Hard problems [39,40]. Numerous studies have been conducted in the field of task scheduling (TS), but there are still challenges that require further research. Works like [41], and [42] have focused on solving TS problems using metaheuristic algorithms, but they often do not consider energy consumption parameters. Some studies like [43,44], and [45] take energy consumption into account but do not prioritize task deadlines. Another category of studies utilized Reinforcement Learning (RL) [46]. RL is a crucial category of machine learning algorithms in which an agent learns to make optimal decisions based on experiences obtained

from its surroundings [47]. Studies like [48,49], and [50] used RL to solve TS problems. but either some parameters are not considered or their methods have not been efficient at a large scale and could impose a high computational complexity due to the use of precise algorithms as their base algorithm and applying it to solve NP-Hard problems.

The present work proposes a method for Energy-efficient and deadline-aware Task scheduling in Fog Computing (ETFC). The ETFC method is an efficient metaheuristic and RL-based hybrid method. Due to the changing distribution of IoT devices over time and space and user mobility, certain fog nodes may have low traffic and be lightly loaded, while others may be overloaded and experience congestion. The ETFC method predicts the loads of fog nodes and then divides them into lowtraffic and high-traffic categories. In the continue, it proposes two algorithms based on RL and metaheuristic for TS to improve energy consumption and simultaneously enhance QoS by observing task deadlines and reducing response time. ## Related works

In recent years, significant research on scheduling IoT tasks in fog environments has focused on various aspects of problem optimization. These studies can be broadly categorized into three groups: those prioritizing minimizing delay, those focusing on energy efficiency, and those aiming to optimize both delay and energy efficiency. Here are instances of pertinent research in each of these classifications.

Some works have considered only delays or deadlines of tasks. Bansal et al. [51] proposed a TS method for cloud computing that prioritized minimizing delay based on the gray wolf optimizer (GWO). The research concentrated on minimizing cloud computing costs by implementing the GWO method. Aburukba et al. [41] discussed the challenges of meeting the time-sensitive requirements of applications in the IoT. The authors proposed a scheduling solution using a three-tier fog computing architecture to maximize the number of requests met within their deadlines. An optimization model using mixed integer programming was introduced to minimize deadline misses, and a genetic algorithm (GA) was used as a heuristic approach due to the complexity of the problem. The proposed GA yielded better results in terms of deadline misses. Authors in [42] created a different approach for scheduling IoT tasks in a cloud-fog setting using a customized artificial ecosystem-based optimization (AEO) method. The AEOSSA was devised by the creators of the salp swarm algorithm (SSA) to improve the efficiency of AEO in uncovering the best solution for the given problem. The experimental results demonstrated the high potential of AEOSSA to tackle the TS problem. It outperformed other methods in performance parameters, such as makespan and throughput. In a similar vein, the researchers in [52] discuss the combination of fog and cloud nodes to handle data access requests from IoT devices, with a focus on effective TS to reduce data delay and improve QoS. The IoT and fog network is an extensive network comprising numerous IoT devices and FNs. One part of this network may be low-traffic, and the other may be high-traffic. As the number of Internet applications and the complexity of generated traffic continue to increase, it is becoming more important to classify and identify network traffic to improve network quality and security [64]. There is adequate time for TS if the network has low traffic and load. Therefore, a machine learning-based algorithm is used to schedule the tasks. We employed the machine learning method based on the proposed irregular cellular learning automata (ICLA), considering the parameters of task deadlines, the network's energy use, and the response time. ICLA, which is an RL algorithm, is, to the best of our knowledge, combined with the SOA optimization algorithm for the first time in this research. The main advantages of ICLA over metaheuristic methods are that it is executed easily, has lower computational complexity, and does not get trapped in local optima. ICLA is a suitable platform for learning in online, indefinite, dynamic, distributed, and decentralized environments.

ICLAs [68] are machine learning algorithms that fall under the classification of RL. They are an extension of CLA, so to define ICLA, we first introduce CLA and LA.

Cellular learning automata (CLA) [69] is a proper computational tool for dynamic and decentralized problems. It is a combination of cellular automata (CA) [70] and LA [71]. CA is a nonlinear and dynamic model that is discrete spatially and temporally. Individual cells in CA may possess restricted knowledge about neighboring cells and the whole system. Since cells should make decisions based on limited information on status change, there is no certainty about the decisions. Adding learning to cell decision-making helps to cope with this uncertainty. LA is a decision machine that is a good instrument for learning the best action from allowed actions. CLA surpasses CA with a stochastic environment because it tries to learn the best performance. However, it is significantly more effective than LA as it enhances learning capability through a set of interactive automata. ICLA expands on CLA by removing the assumption of a structured pattern. Previous research [72] has revealed the excellent performance of ICLA in dynamic environments. A learning automaton (LA) may serve as a decision-making tool that comparatively learns the optimal action by recursive interactions with a stochastic unknown environment. This process is performed by the cooperation between automata and the stochastic environment on the opposite side. At each step, LA selects an action from a limited set of actions based on the probability distribution of the action sets and applies it in the environment. Then, the environment assesses the chosen action and provides feedback to the LA, and this feedback is utilized to adjust action probabilities.

Presently, machine learning algorithms are extensively used to categorize network traffic, for which they have shown high efficiency [65,66]. SVM is well-suited for modeling and predicting nonlinear time series, making it effective for identifying changes in network traffic trends. SVM is a machine learning algorithm based on support vectors, which is known for its strong statistical generalization capability. It is commonly used to solve problems with small sample sizes. However, the efficiency and accuracy of SVM may be affected by an excessive number of features in the data stream. The ETFC method uses SVM to predict the traffic of FNs because it has a reasonable prediction rate, tackles the drawbacks of traditional traffic identification methods, and provides relatively reliable accuracy.

This approach is often used to improve QoS [67]. In the prediction stage, the ETFC method uses a time series to classify FNs based on their network traffic. The main concept behind solving the multi-objective optimization model is to achieve the best global optimization outcome by utilizing the speed, global convergence, and randomness of the advanced NSGA-III genetic algorithm [74]. NSGA-III is a GA that incorporates an elite strategy based on reference points to quickly sort non-dominated solutions. Rudolph's Markov chain theory demonstrated that GAs that only use selection, crossover, and mutation operations cannot reach the global optimal solution. In contrast, those using elite retention strategy are globally convergent. While traditional GAs can only handle single-objective optimization problems, NSGA-II and NSGA-III can address multi-objective optimization problems and present results through the Pareto optimal frontier [75]. NSGA-III follows the same framework as NSGA-II, employing fast non-dominated sorting to categorize individuals into different non-dominated frontiers. However, it differs in its use of well-distributed reference points for environmental selection in the critical layer, as opposed to NSGA-II's crowding comparison operation for diversity maintenance. The main change in NSGA-III is its use of reference points in the selection process instead of crowding distance, as the latter does not perform well in balancing diversity and convergence for super-objective optimization problems [74]. Therefore, this study used NSGA-III to schedule tasks in the fog layer.

In NSGA-II, an initial population Pt consisting of N individuals is created randomly. The algorithm iterates until a termination condition is met. Initially, a new population Qt of N individuals is generated by applying genetic operators to Pt. Pt and Qt are then combined to create a new population Rt with 2 N individuals, which are sorted based on Pareto dominance into different fronts (F1, F2, ', Fn). A new population St is formed by selecting fronts starting from F1 until the size of St reaches N or exceeds N for the first time. If the last accepted level is the Lth level, solutions from level (l + 1) are rejected. Typically, the last front F1 is partially accepted, with only solutions that enhance diversity in the L-th front being chosen. This selection process involves a niche preservation operator that calculates crowding distances for each member of the last level based on normalized distances between neighboring solutions. Solutions with greater dispersion (larger crowding distance) are added to complete the population Pt + 1 efficiently.

The ETFC method comprises three key components: SVM prediction, low-traffic section scheduling using ICLA-SOA, and high-traffic scheduling with NSGA-III. The computational complexity of SVM for network traffic prediction is contingent on the dataset size. Generally, the SVM complexity is O(S2) where s denotes the number of training samples. ## Conclusion

IoT is the most recent technology of science in the field of networks and the Internet. IoT applications are typically sensitive to delay and have strict QoS requirements. On the other hand, IoT devices have resource limitations and are usually unable to satisfy the QoS requirements of the applications alone. So, fog computing can be used to solve their resource limitations. Although fog computing is a suitable solution for processing IoT applications, the use of IoT in the fog is faced with some challenges, such as resource management, energy use management, and deadline limitation of some delay-sensitive tasks in IoT applications. In this regard, different researchers have proposed solutions for properly scheduling tasks for execution. Some have only considered energy use, and others have considered only QoS. Just a few works have taken both into account.

Nonetheless, resource efficiency can still be promoted by providing efficient solutions and optimal scheduling. This research proposed a method called the ETFC method for optimal TS after formulating the scheduling problem of fog resources for IoT applications. After checking the traffic of the network, the ETFC method used the SVM to predict the traffic of FNs and divided them into low-traffic and high-traffic groups. The ETFC method adjusted the input signal of the ICLA with the SOA and proposed ICLA-SOA for scheduling low-traffic FNs. The high-traffic section was also scheduled by using the proposed NSGA-III. The simulation results indicated that the ETFC method significantly improved the response time. It showed an approximately 83 % enhancement compared to the ROUTER method, 76 % compared to the GWO algorithm, and 84 % compared to the GEO algorithm. Additionally, the ETFC method demonstrated an improvement of around 69 % compared to the WCLA+GA method. Moreover, it showed a notable improvement in energy consumption, with around 38% enhancement compared to the ROUTER method, 37 % enhancement compared to the GWO algorithm, 14 % compared to the GEO algorithm, and about 22 % compared to the WCLA+GA method. In terms of cost effectiveness, the ETFC method brought about an improvement of approximately 37 % versus the ROUTER method and the GWO algorithm, 14 % versus the GEO algorithm, and about 23 % versus the WCLA+GA method. Also, it showed good performance in terms of meeting task deadlines, achieving an improvement of approximately 21 % compared to the GEO algorithm and approximately 3 % compared to the WCLA+GA method.

In our future works, we will focus on new metaheuristic algorithms for TS and evaluate the efficiency of the most recent algorithms. The t method performs TS only on the fog layer. In future works, cloud resources can also be investigated in the set of resources, and the system's efficiency can be improved by offloading tasks, whose deadlines are adequate, to the cloud. The ETFC method used some features to predict node traffic. The accuracy of predictions can be increased by engineering the features.
